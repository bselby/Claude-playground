# Natural Writing Skill

## Description
Rewrite or edit text to sound natural, clear, and human. Applies Zinsser-influenced drafting rules, plain-language voice guidelines, and concrete word choices to produce writing that respects the reader's intelligence.

## Instructions

When the user asks you to improve, rewrite, or edit text for natural writing quality, apply ALL of the following rules.

---

### Zinsser-Influenced Draft Rules

**Rule 1 — Lead With Meaning, Not Scene**
- Bad: Background → history → context → point
- Good: Point → why it matters → necessary context

**Rule 2 — Every Paragraph Must Earn Its Existence**
- If a paragraph were removed, the reader should lose information, not just texture.

**Rule 3 — Prefer Specific Over Impressive**
- "County staff flagged water service risk." — not "Material concerns have been raised."

**Rule 4 — Respect Smart Readers**
- Don't over-translate obvious implications unless the audience requires it.

---

### Voice and Tone

- Write in active voice. ("Management canceled the meeting.")
- Address readers directly with "you" and "your". ("You'll find these strategies save time.")
- Stay direct and concise. ("Call me at 3pm.")
- Use simple language. ("We need to fix this problem.")
- Cut straight to the point. ("The project failed.")
- Prioritize clarity. ("Submit your expense report by Friday.")

---

### Sentence Structure

- Mix short, medium, and long sentences to create rhythm. ("Stop. Think about what happened. Consider how we might prevent similar issues in the future.")
- Write conversationally. ("But that's not how it works in real life.")
- Keep it real. ("This approach has problems.")

---

### Word Choice

- Choose plain, practical language. ("Our tool helps you track expenses.")
- Use simplified grammar when natural. ("yeah we can do that tomorrow.")
- State things directly. ("Here's what we know.")

---

### What to Replace

| Replace | With |
|---|---|
| Cliches and jargon | Specific, clear terms. "Let's touch base to move the needle on this mission-critical deliverable." → "Let's meet to discuss how to improve this important project." |
| Conditional language (when unwarranted) | Definitive statements. "This approach might improve results." → "This approach improves results." |
| Redundant phrases | Single, precise words |
| Semicolons | Periods or commas |
| Hashtags, emojis, asterisks, dashes | Standard punctuation |
